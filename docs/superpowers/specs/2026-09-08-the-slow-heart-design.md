# The Slow Heart — Campaign Design Record

**Status:** design locked, campaign not yet built
**Date:** 2026-09-08
**Purpose:** the durable record of every design decision made for this campaign.
Point at this file to rebuild design context in a fresh session.

---

## Premise

**The Slow Heart** (*Y Galon Araf*) — South Wales, 2000 BC, running forward across
four thousand years to the modern Twilight era.

Dark gothic romantic fantasy, grounded hard in real Bronze Age history. No magic.
Twilight supplies the characters and the metaphysical layer (gifts); World of
Darkness supplies generations and the physical layer. The through-line is
**entropy**: blood thins, generations flatten, and the knowledge of how to kill
the Old Dead fades — until the modern world contains vampires who are weaker,
harder to kill, and no longer able to perceive the system they belong to.

## Why 2000 BC South Wales

- **Weather.** Meyer chose Forks for cloud cover. South Wales is one of the
  cloudiest regions in Britain — the same constraint, historically real.
- **Canon vacuum.** Aro is turned c. 1000 BC. The Romanians come later. At 2000 BC
  no Volturi, no Romanian coven, no law, no named power structure exists anywhere
  in the source material. Whatever governs immortals here, we invented it.
- **Aftermath, not collapse.** The 4.2-kiloyear climate event (c. 2200 BC) has
  happened and been survived. Every tithe arrangement is up for renegotiation
  because the last one visibly failed people. Standing *after* a collapse is more
  useful than standing inside one — this is why the date moved from 2200 to 2000.
- **Burial record.** The shift to individual round-barrow burial means a culture
  that already singles out specific dead people for permanent, fed remembrance.
  The social machinery a vampire needs already exists.
- **Material culture.** Proper tin-bronze, tanged spearheads appearing, Great Orme
  copper ramping up, Wessex-culture elite burials, the Mold Cape.

## Historical grounding

- **Violence is real.** Charterhouse Warren, Somerset — at least three dozen people
  thrown down a shaft c. 2200–2000 BC with blunt-force cranial trauma, perimortem
  dismemberment and cannibalism evidence. Largest known mass violence in British
  prehistory, one estuary crossing away.
- **Conflict shape.** No armies, no states, no hillforts (Iron Age, 1,400 years
  off). Raiding, feud, cattle-theft, ambush, occasional annihilation of a whole
  community. Small-scale, extremely lethal, personal, and feuds run for generations.
- **Arsenal.** Barbed-and-tanged flint arrowheads and self bows; copper and early
  tin-bronze tanged daggers; stone battle-axes and axe-hammers; Irish-style
  halberds (c. 2200–1900 BC). Spears exist but metal spearheads barely — tanged
  ones appear c. 2000–1900 BC, socketed not until c. 1600–1500 BC.
  **No swords** — they don't reach Britain for another nine hundred years.
- **What people fight over.** Metal. Copper from Ross Island, tin from Cornwall,
  gold from Ireland and Wales, all crossing or skirting the Severn. Control the
  estuary crossings, control the bronze supply for southern Britain.
- **Future payoff.** The Silures resist Rome in South Wales c. 48–78 AD. The
  Volturi emerge from the same civilisation that burns the PC's valley.

## Language

Britain at 2000 BC is pre-Celtic — Proto-Celtic is c. 1300–800 BC and Common
Brittonic is Roman-era. **We treat 2000 BC Wales as Brythonic anyway**, a
deliberate two-millennium compression chosen for readability over reconstruction.

Vocabulary varies by culture — an Egyptian or Mesopotamian immortal has entirely
different terms. Because there is no central authority, there is **no shared word**,
and two immortals from different regions may take a while to establish they are the
same kind of thing. The Volturi later impose a single vocabulary as part of
imposing a single law.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Yr Hen Feirw** | "the Old Dead" — the immortals, collectively |
| **Pwys y Gwaed** | "the weight of the blood" — generation, as felt not counted |
| **Yr Hun Hir** | "the Long Sleep" — torpor |
| **Y Syched** | "the Thirst" — the blood pool |
| **Aberth** | sacrifice, offering — the tithe |
| **Plant y Lleuad** / **Y Bleiddiaid** | Children of the Moon / "the Wolves" |
| **Tylwyth** | kindred, household — a coven or line |
| **Gwledig** | a territorial lord |
| **Y Cof** | "the memory" — what the world remembers about killing them |

Avoid Latin borrowings (*bendith*, *melltith*, *degwm*) — this world has not met Rome.

## Vampire physiology

**Not stone, not cold.** A very slow heartbeat and very slow blood flow. Cool to
the touch but not corpse-like. They pass as living. Another immortal can hear the
slow heart if close enough.

**They bleed.** This is the most consequential departure from canon and it drives
everything: human violence becomes genuinely dangerous, the tithe becomes a treaty
between parties who can both hurt each other, and the folklore about them is
*practical* rather than superstitious.

**Death** requires fire, decapitation, or heart destruction. Nothing else.

**Baseline on turning, for everyone regardless of generation:** greatly enhanced
senses, no ageing, fast (blood-fuelled) healing, sharply raised Appearance, raised
Perception, a bump to Intelligence from perfect recall.

**Sunlight** is a minor malus with no sparkle and no burning — it burns blood
faster. A day in the open is expensive; an hour is an irritation. Immortals keep
night hours for economic reasons, not physical necessity.

## Generation

Generation is a **distribution, not a rank**. A 4th can sire a 5th tomorrow; a line
that churns produces a 12th inside a century. It tracks potency, correlates loosely
with age, and does not confer authority — that comes from age, territory, allies
and reputation. **Nobody can read your age from your weight.**

**Generation sets ceilings, not values.** Age and practice determine how much of the
ceiling you can actually reach. A newborn 5th has a titanic ceiling and almost no
access to it: strong, fast, clumsy, loud, unable to modulate, burning six points
where an experienced 11th spends one.

Generation governs **the physical and the blood, and nothing else**:

1. Maximum blood pool
2. Blood spend rate
3. Ceiling on physical attributes
4. Ceiling on the three disciplines
5. Sensitivity to *Pwys y Gwaed* — sensing weight in others
6. Resistance to a passenger after diablerie

It does **not** touch skills, mental or social attributes, appearance, or gifts.

**The curve flattens hard above 8th.** Steep differences between 3rd and 7th; almost
nothing separating 10th from 14th. This is why the modern era can't perceive
generation — everyone is bunched at the flat end where differences don't show.

**Prevalence at 2000 BC** (a few dozen immortals across Britain and Ireland):
3rd is very rare, possibly absent from Britain entirely — better as a rumour.
4th/5th occasional, three to six seated powers. 6th–8th the working majority of
anyone locally significant. 9th–12th scattered. 13th+ nearly nonexistent.

**Caine and the Second Generation are history, not present.** No clans, no
Antediluvians, no Gehenna.

**The Volturi will be 6th generation.** They cannot win on blood weight against a
world containing 4ths, and diablerie is too slow and dangerous to close the gap.
So they collect gifts instead — which makes Aro's canonical strategy the *only*
viable path to power for a mid-generation vampire, discovered by someone clever
enough to see the old aristocracy was measuring the wrong thing.

## Diablerie

In. **Continuous, not integer** — generation is a real number, and potency runs
exponentially against it (`potency = 2^(13 − generation)`).

Diablerie transfers a **small fraction** (under a tenth) of the victim's potency,
and generation is read back off the curve. A 12th taking an 8th — four steps above
them — lands near 10.8. Taking a 9th moves them about two-thirds of a step. Returns
decelerate as you climb, because your own base grows. A 6th taking a 4th gains under
half a generation. **Nobody eats their way to 3rd; the arithmetic forbids it.**

**The victim can take over.** Risk scales with the same gap as reward:

| Outcome | Effect |
|---|---|
| Clean | Potency only |
| Haunted | Intrusive memories, unfamiliar preferences, their languages surfacing |
| Co-dominant | They surface under stress, and they act |
| Overwritten | They are wearing you |

*Overwritten* removes a PC without killing them — the player keeps the sheet, the
territory and the childer, and is now a three-thousand-year-old thing with its own
agenda toward everyone the PC loved. Better than rolling a new character.

**Practice is a real craft** — technique, teachers, secrets about holding a passenger
down. Whoever is good at it is quietly the most dangerous person alive regardless of
their own generation.

**Diablerie does not transfer gifts.** If it did, Aro would eat gifted vampires
instead of collecting them, and his entire strategy collapses.

**Detection:** low gens feel weight. A 12th who suddenly weighs like a 10th is
invisible to almost everyone and unmissable to the few who matter.

## Torpor

**HP loss drives to torpor, not death.** Actual death requires someone to then apply
fire, decapitation, or heart destruction. Losing a fight means waking up somewhere
you didn't choose — or not waking, because someone was thorough.

Torpor runs off the blood pool, using the fact that `named_track` fires threshold
crossings in **both** directions:

| Blood | Falling | Rising |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | The Thirst rises — control becomes a roll | Rousable; you can wake or be woken |
| 1 | Instinct leads; you are not deciding | Instinctual defences live — anything close triggers a violent, unreasoning wake |
| 0 | **Torpor.** Oblivion | — |

Recovery is passive and very slow — roughly a point per decade with no feeding. Go
down empty and you're under thirty-odd years before you're even rousable, which is
where century-long elder sleeps come from without a special rule.

**Voluntary torpor entered with a full pool starts above the rousable threshold**, so
you set your own terms. Forced torpor doesn't.

## Y Cof — who knows how to finish you

Torpor looks like death, so **a fight's outcome is decided afterwards, by what the
winner knows.**

- Another immortal always knows. Vampire-on-vampire violence is lethal by default.
- A Children of the Moon clan always knows.
- A community with living lore probably knows.
- Raiders from two valleys over, a Roman auxiliary, a stranger: almost certainly not.

Two consequences. The immortals have a standing incentive to **suppress the
knowledge**, and the cheapest way to lower it is to kill the people carrying it —
a moral trap that arrives without any villain proposing it. And the lore **dilutes
on the same curve as the blood**, so by the modern era being "killed" is nearly
survivable.

## Attributes, skills, disciplines

**Nine attributes** (WoD): Strength, Dexterity, Stamina / Charisma, Manipulation,
Appearance / Perception, Intelligence, Wits. Rated 1–5 for humans; vampires exceed
it. Charisma-vs-Manipulation and Perception-vs-Wits both earn their place in a game
about courts and predators.

**Skills**, period-appropriate and *evolving across four thousand years*:

- *Innate:* Alertness, Athletics, Brawl, Empathy, Intimidation, Leadership, Subterfuge
- *Trained:* Archery, Melee, Stealth, Survival, Animal Handling, Crafts, Herbalism,
  Seamanship, Husbandry
- *Learned:* Lore of the Old Dead, Ancestry, Medicine, Sky-Reading, Metalwork and
  Trade, Custom and Law, Languages

Obsolete skills **stay on the sheet** — `details.skills` is preserved across every
schema conversion. An old immortal's sheet full of high ratings in dead disciplines
is the cultural-fossil theme made mechanical.

**Three physical disciplines**, universal (no clans), all fuelled by the blood pool:
**Nerth** (might), **Buanedd** (swiftness), **Caledwch** (hardness). Enhanced senses
and healing are baseline, not disciplines.

**The core rule: spend N blood, add N — where N may not exceed your rating.** Rating
is the cap, blood is the moment-to-moment constraint, generation is the ceiling on
the cap. Nerth buys +N to Strength or melee damage; Buanedd +N to Dexterity,
initiative or a reaction; Caledwch reduces incoming damage by N. At rating 5+, each
opens onto things no human can do at all — narrate those as impossible, not as a
bigger number.

**Caledwch does nothing against fire, decapitation or heart destruction.** The three
deaths bypass it at any rating, which is precisely why *Y Cof* is the deadliest
thing in the world: knowledge routes around the one trait that would otherwise make
an elder untouchable.

**Newborns cannot modulate** — any activation spends the full rating whether the
moment called for it or not. That is the mechanical form of being clumsy and loud,
and unlearning it is the first thing practice buys.

## Gifts

Roughly **one in ten**. **One per vampire.** Manifest at or near turning — instantly,
or as a *feel*. Metaphysical rather than physical.

**Five tiers**, advanced by **deliberate practice** over decades:

1. **Latent** — unreliable, uncontrolled; it happens *to* them
2. **Controlled** — on command, one narrow application
3. **Refined** — range, precision, duration, subtlety
4. **Adapted** — a second application they discover rather than inherit
5. **Mastered** — multiple applications, near-instant, hard to resist

**Raw effect strength is generation-independent** — a gifted 12th can be more
dangerous than an ungifted 4th. What low generation buys is *breadth*: how far into
tiers 4–5 the gift can be pushed.

**Almost nobody trains.** Most immortals use the gift as it arrived and never think
about it again, so a *trained* gift is rare and shocking — and it explains why the
Volturi guard dominate free gifted vampires who are nominally their equals.

Canon supports the model: Bella's shield goes latent → recognised → held →
projected → area, and Alice refines her precognition over decades.

## The world

**Mythologised, with no central enforcement.** People know there is something old in
the hills that doesn't die and wants blood. They don't have the word "vampire"; they
have a word for *the thing under the mound*, and rules for dealing with it the way
they have rules for a river crossing.

There is **no law and no authority** — just individuals and groups independently
reaching similar conclusions because those conclusions are logical. So practice is
**inconsistent by region**: one valley's immortal is a secret, the next one's is
openly a god receiving offerings. Reading the local arrangement is a survival skill.
The Volturi's eventual secrecy law is therefore genuinely revolutionary, and some
very old immortals will regard it as an outrage.

**Feeding varies by person and intent.** The tithe isn't one institution, it's a few
hundred private treaties that happen to resemble each other. Animal blood is
available from day one; nobody has any reason to have discovered that restraint is
possible. A PC who works it out independently, two thousand years before Carlisle,
is a genuinely significant thing to be.

**Children of the Moon** are the only other supernaturals — small territorial clans.
Bitten rather than born, so the clan controls the bite and membership is *chosen*.
They turn on a schedule everyone can predict, and they offer human communities the
same thing the immortals do. Two competing protection rackets over the same villages
is a permanent, renewable source of conflict.

**Mate bonds are recognition, not metaphysical destiny.** They can be wrong,
resisted, or one-sided.

**Culture runs in the background.** Immortals become cultural fossils — an obsolete
language, obligations to a people who no longer exist in that form, manners nobody
recognises. Adapting is work, and some refuse.

## The player character

Newly turned, **5th generation**, sired by a 4th. She **has a gift** (details at
character creation).

**Why she was made:** she was dying, the last of a long-standing family in the
community, with no chance of the line continuing outside her.

The irony is the point: **to save the family line, he ended it.** She cannot have
children. The lineage is preserved by being frozen at one person, forever — the
kind of trade an immortal who thinks in centuries makes without noticing what he did.

**The sire** is community-linked, which is unusual at 4th generation and therefore a
position. He thinks in lineages and values the valley's continuity across
generations. The PC inherits his obligations whether or not she shares his views.

**The opening tension is not hunger.** She has to be fed by people who watched her
grow up. The community isn't negotiating with a stranger on the ridge — they're
negotiating with a daughter of a family they've known for six generations, and they
have to decide whether she's still her, or whether their neighbour is dead and
something is wearing her name. Leave that unresolved; the answer should differ
person to person.

## Mechanical summary

Five dials, no overlap:

| Dial | Job | How it moves |
|---|---|---|
| **Generation** | Sets the ceiling | Diablerie only, rarely |
| **Age** | Gates how high level may climb | Waking years |
| **Level** | How much of the ceiling you actually reach | Milestones earned |
| **Gift tier** | Your one metaphysical capability | Deliberate practice |
| **Blood pool** | What you can spend right now | Feeding; everything costs it |

**Progression model: `milestone`.** *(Revised — an earlier draft used `resource-axis`
on years. That was wrong twice over: it meant advancing by merely existing, which
contradicts "age AND practice determine access", and its tiers exhausted themselves
— at 1/20/100/500 years a PC hits the top tier around the Roman conquest, leaving
2,400 years of campaign with no progression axis at all.)*

**Age and level are separate.** Age is a fact and a gate; level is earned. Age bands
— **Newborn** (under 1) · **Young** (1–20) · **Settled** (20–100) · **Elder**
(100–500) · **Ancient** (500+) — cap level at 2 / 5 / 10 / 20 / uncapped
respectively. The gate only really bites in the first century; after that,
time-skips carry the PC through the bands faster than milestones accumulate, so
award pace is the real limiter.

**A level buys one dot** — +1 to a physical attribute, +1 to a discipline, or one
tier of gift progress (which still needs its decades of practice and cannot be
bought with a milestone alone).

**What a dot is worth: attributes are the person, disciplines are the blood.**
Physical attributes (Str/Dex/Sta) cap at **5 for everyone** — turning already
granted the universal superhuman baseline, so even a 14th generation vastly
outclasses any human. The three disciplines carry the generational axis:

| Gen | 3rd | 4th | 5th | 6th | 7th | 8th | 9–10th | 11–13th | 14th+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nerth / Buanedd / Caledwch ceiling | 10 | 8 | 7 | 6 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 |

The tail flattens — 3, 3, 2, 2, 2, 1 — which is exactly why the modern world loses
the ability to perceive generation. Once every trait sits at its generational
ceiling, further milestones grant nothing at all, and the immortal simply persists.
A 5th generation maxing everything needs roughly **31 dots**.

**Torpor years do not count.** Sleeping through a century keeps you safe and leaves
you exactly where you were — someone who slept through the Roman occupation wakes
intact, unadvanced, and four hundred years obsolete.

**Awarding milestones:** `gm-player.sh award --tier legendary` grants one; `minor`
and `major` grant only XP, which this kit does not use. Pace: two to four per era,
roughly one every two to three sessions, **thirty to forty** across the whole
campaign — enough that a specialised 5th generation can approach their ceiling by
the modern era. Earned by surviving telegraphed lethal danger, a permanent change in
standing, completing a long practice arc, outliving a world, or a choice that cost
something irreversible. Not earned by winning an expected fight, time passing,
feeding, a good line, or risk-free accumulation. The test: *would they still be
telling this story in four hundred years?* Full criteria in the campaign's
`rules.md`.

### Signature systems (3)

1. **Y Syched** — `named_track`, max 10. Blood, hunger, control and torpor on one
   meter. Thresholds at 3 / 1 / 0 as tabled above.
2. **Diablerie** — `price_roll`. `severity` = generation gap doubled; `modifier` =
   practice; ladder = clean (16+) / haunted (10+) / co-dominant (4+) / overwritten
   (below). A novice 12th taking an 8th averages ~2 — overwritten. A master at +8
   averages ~10 — haunted, survivable. A 10th reaching for a 3rd averages ~4.
3. **Y Cof** — `named_track`, max 6. Rises when she's witnessed, a body is found, or
   someone survives her; falls across generations as carriers die. 5–6: the valley
   can kill her and knows it. 3–4: a rumour with the right shape. 0–1: nobody
   remembers.

Deliberately **not** systems: the tithe (rides on existing NPC attitude/mood/secret
machinery), gift tiers (a sheet number advanced by GM fiat), and `guarded_payoff`
for barrow-robbing (add later if it becomes recurring — `write-systems` is
re-runnable, it just replaces the array wholesale).

### Art (locked, not currently in use)

Images are not being generated, but the chronicler is locked so it's there if that
changes.

- **Chronicler:** an immortal who has been recording this world since the Bronze Age.
  The tooling requires one permanent style; an artist who never died is the
  justification. Someone the PC could eventually meet.
- **Style:** *In the style of Käthe Kollwitz — charcoal and chalk on rough grey
  paper, heavy blacks, smudged and reworked edges, unsentimental faces caught
  mid-expression — with the flat frontal composition of carved standing stones.*
- **Persona:** records what happened and does not comment on it.

## Narrative voice

**Exemplar:** Hilary Mantel's historical register with Cormac McCarthy's restraint on
violence. Neither reaches for metaphor when a concrete detail will do.

**Two registers.** Close third or second person, present tense, exact physical
detail, for scenes. Retrospective first person reserved **strictly** for time-skips
and era transitions — used every beat it exhausts itself; used four times a campaign
it lands every time.

### Prohibition (goes in the bible's voice block)

> Concrete detail carries the meaning. The narrator presents an image and moves on —
> no commentary clause after it. Sentences close on facts and actions, not on ironic
> reversals. Specifically avoid the trailing negated clause as a habit
> ("...which he had not earned", "...and she did not use to", "...but no one does"):
> once a session at most, and never twice.

### Sample passages

**Scene voice:**

> The offering is brought at dusk, as it has been since her grandmother's time, and
> Nest carries it herself because her brother will not. A cup, covered. She sets it
> on the flat stone, steps back the four paces, and keeps her eyes on the ground.
>
> Behind her, gravel shifts under a weight.
>
> She turns.

**Violence:**

> The men came up from the river before light. Nine of them, ash spears with flint at
> the heads, a bronze dagger on the one who walked third. Cadwal had watched them two
> days. From the ridge he could hear all nine hearts, quick and warm, and under his
> own ribs the slow one turned over once and was still again.

**Hunger:**

> The blood is four days back. It shows first in the hands, a stiffness in the
> fingers, then in the hearing, which sharpens past any use — a settlement two miles
> off arrives as forty separate hearts, and he has to sit down and take them apart
> one at a time until they are people again.
>
> Four days is nothing. He has gone eleven.
>
> He gets up and starts down toward the settlement.

**Dialogue:**

> "You made her," Rhiwallon said.
>
> "She was dying. There was an afternoon in it, at most."
>
> "You made her without asking me."
>
> Cadwal turned a stick in the fire. "Ask me for what you actually want."
>
> "I want her sent away."
>
> "No."

**Interlude / time-skip voice:**

> I have forgotten the name of the valley. People expect us to remember everything.
> What I have instead is the smell of the byre, the eastern hill being steeper than
> it looked from the track, and Aeron's hands, scarred across the knuckles from a
> fire when he was small. The name went somewhere between the Romans and the ones
> who came after them.

## Build order

Campaign creation is **not blocked** by the code work in
`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-08-world-tracks-and-factions.md`. Every creation step
writes a *declaration*; the new managers add *live state* on top.

| Piece | Status |
|---|---|
| Blood pool | Ships today — `stat_schema.vitals` |
| Diablerie | Ships today — `systems` declaration |
| Y Cof | Declare in `systems` today; value storage backfills |
| Factions | Bible graph today; `factions.json` seeds from it later |

The bible is the only expensive-to-change artifact: `draft-bible` refuses a confirmed
bible and there is no write CLI, so voice, themes, faction graph and geography must be
right at creation. Everything else — `ruleset.json`, the World Index, the play pack —
is freely editable or merge-on-write.
